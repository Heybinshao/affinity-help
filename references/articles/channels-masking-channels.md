3.   Masking from channels

Create masks based on channel information to selectively apply and mask adjustments and filters to your compositions.

Masking using channel information is a great alternative to working with selections, where parts of compositions can be fine-tuned with more precision. Extracting pixel information from only certain areas is one area where masking from channels can help. This is particularly true if the regions to extract pixel information from are complex and where other methods of working (using Flood Select or Selection Brush tools, say) take too long or aren't accurate enough.

1.   On the **Channels** panel, select the channel that upon preview (when clicked) provides the most contrasting image.
2.   **^(ctrl)**-click (Mac) / **right**-click (Windows) the selected channel, then choose **Load to Pixel Selection**. 
3.   On the **Pixel** menu, select **New Mask Layer > Mask Layer**.
4.   On the **Channels** panel, toggle visibility of Red, Green and Blue Channels back on.
5.   On the **Layers** panel, select the image mask layer and apply an adjustment or filter, as required.
6.   Drag the adjustment or filter layer and offer it to the thumbnail of the image layer.

Adding an Adjustment or Filter limits its effect to the area selected based on channel information. Alternatively, your can apply an inverted mask to target areas of interest.

You can also include an inverted selection mask to target specific and complex areas.

1.   Select the mask layer.
2.   On the **Pixel** menu, select **Invert**.

If the Channels panel isn't shown, on the **Window** menu select **Pixel > Channels**.
