3.   Inpainting

Inpainting lets you seamlessly remove or replace unwanted areas of an image — from distracting objects like power lines and lamp posts to dust spots, scratches, and damaged areas — using algorithms that intelligently fill the gap using surrounding image content.

![Image 1: After](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/2Tb6Z1epBXMnlCOX90iTce/af042e2d8f2003dc078e81f763a97f57/tool_inpainting02_after.jpg)

After removing a lampost from a landscape image.

![Image 2: Before](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/EMhDRbmHcZXyzD7Pqtmh4/9aebe5588381dccb4f7440642dcd82cd/tool_inpainting02_before.jpg)

Base image.

For example, you can remove or replace:

*   Power lines and other obstructions
*   People and animals
*   Vignetting
*   Lens flares
*   Background details
*   Dust spots and scratches
*   Stuck or "hot" pixels

The **Inpainting Brush Tool** is used to paint over (and identify) damaged or unwanted areas. Complex algorithms then take over to harvest information from the surrounding areas of the image in order to reconstruct the missing data.

Inpainting is particularly useful for repairing physically damaged printed photos which have been subsequently digitized.

Upon the inpainting process, the selected RAW or image layer is rasterized. This behavior can be modified via the app's **Settings** in the **Assistant** options section.

1.   Use the **Layers** panel to select a layer to work on.
2.   Select the **Inpainting Brush Tool**![Image 3](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/4gyLcvBccl4fKXeKrp1JSW/13bc2adfbd5dacd715336811593430e7/inpainting_brush_tool.svg) .
3.   The tool uses a soft-round brush by default. To use a different brush style, choose one from the **Brushes** panel.
4.   Adjust the context toolbar settings.
5.   Drag across the image to identify the area of lost or damaged data.

If compositing with multiple layers in project, inpainting may be performed on one of the top layers in the stack. Consider changing **Current Layer** setting on the top **Context Toolbar** to **Current Layer and Below**. This ensures that the pixel information is gathered from all layers under the selected one for optimal results.

1.   Use the **Layers**panel to select a layer to work on.
2.   Using a selection tool, select the area you want to remove or replace.
3.   On the **Pixel**menu, choose **Inpaint**.

This method is useful when you need precise control over the inpainted region — for example, when the area to remove has a well-defined shape that is easier to select than to paint over freehand.

*   Zoom in close to the area you wish to inpaint.
*   Set a suitable brush size from the context toolbar.
*   If you are painting out people or complex shapes, try painting over gaps (e.g., inbetween legs and arms) as well for better consistency.
*   For precise removals, establish a selection around the target area first and use **Pixel > Inpaint**instead of painting freehand — this gives you greater control over which pixels are replaced.
*   Remember you can always do multiple passes if the result of the inpainting does not look authentic or seamless enough. Simply paint over the areas again.

*   To change brush size, use the **[** or **]** key.
