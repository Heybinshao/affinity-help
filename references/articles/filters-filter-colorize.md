The Colorize filter enables you to colorize old monochromatic or faded images, typically captured with film cameras in the pre-digital era—although it's not limited to these scenarios. The initial results generated by the filter are adjustable and often provide an excellent starting point for more advanced photo restoration workflows.

The **Colorization** model must be downloaded prior to using the feature. The model is available in **Settings > Machine Learning Models** section.

Affinity's Machine Learning functionality is not generative and advocates human creativity.

This filter is applied as a destructive filter.

For a non-destructive workflow, apply the filter to a duplicate layer rather than the Background layer.

Sometimes when launching the filter, Affinity's Assistant may inform you that it cannot be applied due to existing color in the image. This is common, as old photographs naturally develop subtle color casts when fading or reacting to environmental factors. In these cases, simply convert your photograph to black and white before applying the filter.

Another useful editing tip is to increase contrast before applying the filter, as old photographs naturally fade over time. You may find that applying Colorize to images with enhanced contrast produces more natural-looking results. Contrast can be increased using Brightness/Contrast, Levels, or Curves adjustments, as required.

Access the filter from the **Pixel > Filters > Colors** menu.

The following settings can be adjusted in the filter dialog:

*   **Saturation**—increases or decreases color saturation.
*   **Temperature**—increases or decreases the color temperature.
