2.   Compositing

Compositing lets you merge images and layers to build polished, unified visuals. Use it to blend elements, fine-tune lighting and color, and create everything from realistic photo edits to imaginative digital artwork.

![Image 1: Composite example of a surreal, cinematic scene.](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/3rbiA2QBUAxg90QVbHTGE5/f5134c516014a6814bb1b25247670be8/composite_example.jpg)

Composite example of a surreal, cinematic scene.

Compositing is built on the idea of stacking and blending multiple visual elements to create a unified scene. Each item—photos, illustrations, textures, or effects—lives on its own layer. By adjusting how those layers interact, you can control what’s visible, how light behaves, and how elements blend together. Tools like masks, blend modes, and opacity settings let you hide, reveal, or merge details with precision. As you fine-tune these interactions, separate pieces gradually form a cohesive final image.

Affinity conveniently organises all the tools and panels you need for compositing workflows in its own **Compositing Studio**

The most common workflows for compositing include managing and editing of the following:

*   **Layers—**Every element you add sits on its own layer. Keeping layers organized makes it easier to edit individual parts without affecting the rest of your artwork.
*   **Layer Masks—**Masks let you hide or reveal areas of a layer without permanently erasing anything. They’re essential for creating smooth transitions, removing backgrounds, or blending subjects into a new environment.
*   **Blend Modes—**Blend modes control how one layer interacts with the light and color of the layer beneath it. They help create realistic lighting effects, color harmonies, and artistic blends.
*   **Opacity and Fill—**Adjusting opacity lets you soften elements or make them partially transparent. This helps integrate textures, shadows, and highlights more naturally.
*   **Adjustments—**Color and tone adjustments—such as brightness, contrast, hue, and temperature—help match elements so they feel like part of the same scene.

*   **Background Replacement—**Swap out an entire backdrop to place your subject in a different setting. Masks and edge-refinement tools help it look natural.
*   **Exposure Merging—**Combine multiple photos shot at different brightness levels to preserve detail in both shadows and highlights.
*   **Shadow and Light Matching—**Add or adjust shadows and highlights so all elements share consistent lighting direction and intensity.
*   **Texture and Overlay Effects—**Layer textures, patterns, or atmospheric elements (like smoke, light leaks, or fog) to enrich the mood.
*   **Object Removal—**Use compositing to blend surrounding pixels or other sources to seamlessly fill missing or removed content.

*   **Match lighting first—**Angle, intensity, and color temperature should feel consistent across all elements.
*   **Check perspective—**Horizons, vanishing points, and focal lengths should align.
*   **Blend edges carefully—**Feathered masks and subtle transitions avoid harsh cutouts.
*   **Color-grade at the end—**Applying a final global adjustment helps unify every layer.
*   **Zoom out often—**Evaluating at full view helps you spot mismatches in scale or lighting.
*   **Keep shadows believable—**Softness and darkness should match the environment’s light source.
