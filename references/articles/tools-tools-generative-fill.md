Affinity's Generative Fill Tool uses Canva AI to generate content based on your prompts. Results will vary depending on the complexity of the scene and how descriptive your prompt is — the more context you give, the better the output. For best results, use clear, specific language that describes what you want to add or replace, including details about style, color, and surroundings where relevant.

The Generative Fill Tool adds a layer with a mask to the Layers panel. This supports a non-destructive workflow, allowing you to refine the fill by brushing content in or out. If you're unfamiliar with masking and brush-based refinement, see the [Layer masking — How to edit a pixel mask](https://www.affinity.studio/help/layers-layer-masks/) topic for guidance.

Unlike generating a standalone image, Generative Fill works within an existing scene — so your prompt should describe what you want to appear in the selected area, with enough context to help it blend naturally with its surroundings.

*   Describe what should fill the selected area, not what was there before.
*   Reference surrounding context where relevant — mention the lighting, surface, material, or environment so the result sits naturally in the scene.
*   Include style details such as mood, texture, color palette, or finish.
*   Be specific rather than vague — "a weathered red brick wall with uneven mortar" will outperform "a wall".
*   For photo editing, consider the conditions of the shot: indoor or outdoor, time of day, weather, and depth.

*   "Smooth sandy beach continuing the shoreline, warm afternoon light, soft wave textures at the edge."
*   "Dense forest floor with dappled sunlight filtering through, consistent with the surrounding woodland."
*   "A clear, overcast sky with soft cloud detail, matching the cool natural light in the rest of the image."
*   "Flat geometric pattern in muted blue and warm gray tones, minimal style, consistent with the surrounding design."
*   "Polished concrete floor continuing the surface, matching the even studio lighting above."

*   Generative Fill works best on small to medium selections. For large areas, consider breaking the selection into smaller sections and filling each separately for more controlled results.
*   The more precisely you select the area you want to fill, the more accurately the tool can match the surrounding content. Use selection refinement tools to tighten your selection before applying.
*   If the result isn't what you expected, try regenerating — AI outputs vary with each attempt, and a subsequent generation may better match your intent.
*   Adjust your prompt and try again if initial results are off. Adding more detail about lighting, texture, material, or surroundings often produces a stronger match.
*   Use the mask added to the Layers panel to brush out unwanted parts of the generated result and blend it more naturally with the rest of your image.
*   For removing objects, ensure the selection fully covers the subject and includes a small amount of the surrounding area to give the tool enough context to reconstruct the background convincingly.

*   Generative Fill may produce inconsistent results on highly detailed or complex areas, such as intricate textures, repeating patterns, or precise geometric structures.
*   Generating recognizable faces, specific people, or text within the fill area may not produce reliable or accurate results.
*   Results are AI-generated and therefore non-deterministic — outputs will differ between attempts even with the same prompt and selection.
*   Very large selections may produce results that lack detail or appear inconsistent across the filled area.
*   The tool requires a descriptive prompt to produce targeted results. Vague or empty prompts may generate content that doesn't suit the scene.

The following options can be adjusted on the context toolbar:

*   **Prompt**—the text or description you enter to tell Canva AI what you want it to create or change.
*   **Image count**—determines how many images are rendered on output with the max number of eight.
*   **Allow generation beyond selection**—when checked (default), the image fill process analyses the scene around the selection patch. When off, it restricts the process to the selection area.
