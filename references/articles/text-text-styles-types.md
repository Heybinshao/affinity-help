3.   Text style types

There are three types of text style in Affinity: Paragraph, Character, and Group. They are used for different purposes.

There are no restrictions on what text attributes and properties can be applied to a text style, regardless of its type. Instead, the type determines the predominant purpose of that text style:

*   **Paragraph**—used to define the properties of paragraphs.
*   **Character**—used to define the properties of individual glyphs (or words, lines, and sentences inside a paragraph).
*   **Group**—used to define common settings for a group of text styles. Group styles are not applied directly to text.

Although text styles are assigned a type, Affinity gives you the option of applying paragraph and character text styles flexibly. For example, you can apply paragraph styles to selected glyphs as if they are character styles, without affecting the style applied to the overall paragraph.

If a keyboard shortcut is assigned to a text style, that shortcut will apply the style using its defined type. For example, if you select a portion of a paragraph and press a paragraph style's shortcut, the style is applied to the whole paragraph as a paragraph style, rather than as a character style to the selection.

A **group** text style defines a foundation on which other text styles are built and allows you to quickly create a text style hierarchy.

Group text styles are listed only on the **Text Styles** panel and selecting them does nothing. It's easiest to see the relationships between styles when the panel's hierarchical view is enabled.

A group style can be considered as its group's **parent** or **master** text style. Other styles based on it are considered to be **child** or **subordinate** styles.

An existing text style can be made a subordinate text style by setting its **Based on** attribute to a group style.

The complexity of your text style hierarchy is entirely in your hands. Subordinate styles can also be master paragraph, character, or group styles with their own subordinates.

1.   On the **Text Styles** panel, select **Edit "<style name>"** on the required style's options menu ![Image 1](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/2XLuXTwmEh714qaYONrPKP/6d26123960257b3233a3eb084188eda7/moremenuicon.svg) .
2.   On the dialog that appears: 
    1.   Select **Style**.
    2.   Select the required **Type**: **Character**, **Paragraph**, or **Group**.
    3.   Click **OK**.

1.   On the **Text Styles** panel, select **Edit "<style name>"** on the required style's options menu ![Image 2](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/2XLuXTwmEh714qaYONrPKP/6d26123960257b3233a3eb084188eda7/moremenuicon.svg) .
2.   On the dialog that appears: 
    1.   Select **Style**.
    2.   Enable **Show in both panels**.
    3.   Click **OK**.

The style can then be applied as a character style via the context toolbar or the **Character** panel.

*   On the **Text Styles** panel, select **Show Hierarchical** on the **Panel Preferences** menu ![Image 3](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/6xHYQYa3ePyLnNbDC3c5Ey/56d26d75e40a2d195e578a41b2b16612/panel_preferences.svg) .

1.   On the **Text Styles** panel, select **Create Group Style**![Image 4](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/5GILikO6vN1JIy1uVv0efy/3e3309bf5bd7bf421fdf174264861170/CreateGroupStyle.svg) .
2.   On the dialog that appears: 
    1.   Adjust the settings as required.
    2.   Click **OK**.

3.   Create a subordinate style: 
    1.   On the Text Styles panel, select **Create Style Based on** on your group style's options menu ![Image 5](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/2XLuXTwmEh714qaYONrPKP/6d26123960257b3233a3eb084188eda7/moremenuicon.svg) .
    2.   On the dialog that appears, adjust the settings as required, then click **OK**.

The subordinate style's **Type** is initially **Group**, but it can be changed to **Character** or **Paragraph**.

Further subordinate text styles can be created from the group style or one of its existing subordinates.
