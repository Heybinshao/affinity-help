# Color Grading - Affinity Help Center

> 官方来源：https://www.affinity.studio/help/color-grading-color-grade/

1.   [Help Center](https://www.affinity.studio/help/)
2.   Color Grading

Color grading helps you shape the mood and style of your photo by adjusting its colors and tones for a more polished, expressive look.

![Image 1: After color grading the scene with LUT and Selective Color.](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/64NmGmdMFtrOXJcYveRKZf/175be9790b07ac6419f24ebb29cb0431/clrGrading_after.jpg)

After color grading the scene with LUT and Selective Color.

Color grading becomes much easier once you understand how each tool contributes to the final look. Whether you’re correcting color with White Balance, shaping contrast with the Tone Curve, or fine-tuning specific hues through HSL, each adjustment helps you build toward a cohesive style. Creative tools like Color Wheels, Split Toning, and LUTs let you push your images further, while selective edits and histograms help ensure precision and consistency. Together, these techniques give you full control over how your photo feels and how its colors work together.

Affinity conveniently organises all the tools and panels you need for compositing workflows in its own **Color Grading Studio**

The most common tools for color grading include:

*   **White Balance (Temperature & Tint)**Adjusts the overall color cast of your photo. Use it to correct colors that look too warm (yellow/orange) or too cool (blue/green), ensuring the image looks natural and true to life.
*   **Tone Curve / RGB Curves**A powerful tool for shaping the brightness and contrast of your image. You can lift or deepen shadows, refine midtones, and adjust highlights. In RGB mode, it also allows precise color control through individual Red, Green, and Blue channels.
*   **HSL (Hue, Saturation, Luminance) / Color Mixer**Lets you adjust specific color ranges independently—like blues in the sky or greens in foliage. You can shift a color’s hue, increase/decrease its intensity, or brighten/darken it without affecting the rest of the image.
*   **Color Wheels (Shadows / Midtones / Highlights)**Provides targeted color adjustments based on tonal areas. Add cool tones to shadows, warm up highlights, or fine-tune midtones to give your image a balanced or stylized look.
*   **Split Toning / Color Grading (Highlights & Shadows Tinting)**Adds color to the highlights and shadows independently to create a specific mood or style. Commonly used for cinematic looks—for example, warm highlights with cool shadows.
*   **LUTs / Presets**Predefined “looks” you can apply to your photo in one click. Used to quickly achieve styles such as film emulation, vintage tones, or high-contrast color grades. You can adjust the strength to fine-tune the effect.
*   **Local Adjustments (Masking / Selective Color Tools)**Allows you to apply edits to only part of your photo—such as the sky, subject, or background. Useful for correcting skin tones, enhancing certain colors, or isolating creative effects without affecting the whole image.
*   **Exposure & Contrast Controls**Used to set the overall brightness and contrast before fine-tuning color. Proper exposure helps ensure colors look accurate and remain consistent throughout your edits.
*   **Color Match / Color Transfer**Matches the colors of your photo to another reference image or a selected palette. Helpful for keeping a consistent look across multiple photos or replicating a specific style.
*   **Scopes / Histogram**Visual tools that help you monitor exposure and color balance. They don’t change the image directly but help you identify issues like clipped highlights, crushed shadows, or color bias.

1.   **Fix the basics**
    *   In Develop Studio, on the **Basic**panel, adjust Exposure, White Balance, Contrast, Shadows/Highlights.

2.   **Shape contrast & tone**
    *   In Develop Studio, on the **Tones**panel, apply Tone Curve.
    *   Create an S-curve for balanced contrast.
    *   Use RGB Curves to subtly tint tonal ranges.

3.   **Refine specific colors**
    *   In Pixel Studio, target key ranges (skin, sky, foliage).
    *   Adjust Hue, Saturation, Lightness using the respective adjustments.

4.   **Apply creative grading**
    *   In Pixel Studio, use adjustment Layers, such as Split Toning for stylized highlights/shadows, Selective Color for fine color control, and Color Balance for quick global casts.

5.   **Add LUTs if desired**
    *   Pixel>New Adjustment Layer>LUT
    *   Load LUT and lower Opacity to blend naturally.

6.   **Use masks for precision**
    *   Add Mask Layers to apply adjustments only where needed (subject, sky, foreground).
    *   Use Brush, Gradient, or Selection tools to refine.

7.   **Check exposure & balance**
    1.   Enable Histogram panel to avoid clipping.
    2.   Zoom out to evaluate overall color harmony.

8.   **Save your look**
    1.   Export adjustments as a LUT or Macro.
    2.   Save a .af file for non-destructive re-editing.
