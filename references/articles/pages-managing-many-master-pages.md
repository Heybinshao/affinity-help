# Managing many master pages - Affinity Help Center

3.   Managing many master pages

Use a dedicated document as a library to collate all of your master pages. By keeping a main copy of them in one place, they become easy to maintain and reuse.

You might have stored many or all of your master pages in each of your documents. The Pages panel can be difficult to manage if your document contains too many master pages. It's better to only store the master pages you really need in each document.

You can't import a master page into another document on its own, but when you import publication pages, Affinity also imports any master pages they depend on.

You'll need at least one Affinity document to use as a master pages library. This document will eventually contain publication pages with the required master pages applied to them.

The "library" document is a single place to store and maintain all your master pages.

1.   Create a document you want to use as a master pages library.
2.   Do one of the following:
    *   Import pages from any existing documents that contain the master pages you want to consolidate. (On the **Document**menu, select **Pages > Add from File.**)
    *   Add new master pages from scratch. (Refer to the "Editing master pages" topic for details.)

3.   If you imported pages, delete any publication pages that contain content you don't want to keep.
4.   Use the master pages to create fresh, unedited publication pages. (On the **Pages**panel, drag one at a time from the **Master Pages**section and drop on the **Pages**section.) These act as clean references for each master; you'll import using their page numbers, not the master pages' names.

You might choose to maintain separate library documents to represent different page categories. For example, a magazine might have separate documents for tutorial, feature, and news pages. The import workflow below works the same way for each.

Affinity lets you select publication pages to import, but not master pages directly. Any masters applied to publication pages are imported automatically.

1.   Open the document containing your library of master pages.
2.   In the document view, find the master page design you want to use, then click within it.
3.   Look at the **Pages**panel and make a note of the relevant page numbers for the design you want to use—at least the page numbers indicated as the active page or spread, depending on how many consecutive pages make up the master page.
4.   Switch to your working document.
5.   On the **Document**menu, select **Pages > Add from File**.
6.   Navigate to the library document, select it, then select **Open**.
7.   On the window that appears:
    1.   Select **Page Numbers**and type the page numbers you identified earlier, e.g. _1,2,3,4_ to import specific page numbers or _1-4_ to import a consecutive range.
    2.   Set the position of the imported pages:
        1.   **Add pages**—choose between **Before**, **After**, **After last page**, or **Replacing**your specified page number or section.
        2.   **Pages or section**—select **Pages**for placement at a precise page number, or **Section**to place them at a section boundary.
        3.   **Pages**or **Section**—type a specific page number or select a section name.

8.   Enable **Ignore Initial Section**and **Discard Unused Text Styles**to avoid importing unused or unwanted content.
9.   Select **OK**.

After importing, the working document contains:

*   The publication pages you identified.
*   Any master pages applied to the publication pages.
*   Text styles used on those pages.

You can create additional pages or spreads from the imported master page.

If the imported pages landed in the wrong position, move them—drag their thumbnails to the correct position on the **Pages** panel, or **^(ctrl)-click** (Mac) / right-click (Windows) a thumbnail and select **Move/Copy Pages** and specify the correct position.
