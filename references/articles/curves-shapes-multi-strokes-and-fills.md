3.   Using multiple strokes and fills

In Affinity you can add a number of separate strokes and fills to the same selected object. These can be based on gradients or solid colors.

This feature, combined with the ability to alter the transparency and blend mode for each stroke and fill applied, can be used to produce a range of different color interactions between upper and lower strokes and fills.

By setting a decreasing stroke width on strokes higher up in the stack, you can easily create interesting decoupage effects, which work especially well with varying blend modes (for example, the erase mode).

4.   On the created stroke or fill entry, click the color selector ring, stroke width and/or blend mode to edit.

*   With an object selected, click on a stroke or fill from within the **Appearance** panel.

When a fill or stroke is made active, this will be denoted by a white dot to the left of that stroke/fill on the **Appearance** panel. This fill or stroke can then be edited in panel, or directly via the **Color** and/or **Stroke** panels.

*   Drag and drop strokes and fills within the **Appearance** panel.

Strokes and fills in the panel list are arranged by z-order (like layers in a layer stack) and can be dragged and dropped to reposition them within the panel. The topmost stroke or fill shows in front of others.

The **Stroke** panel's **Order** option can also be used to place the active stroke either in front of/behind the object's fill(s).

The **Alignment** option controls where the stroke is positioned in relation to the object outline and other strokes. Initially, keep the alignment setting the same for each stroke, then experiment with them once you gain experience.

To keep underlying stroke's visible, ensure stroke widths increase on each stroke entry the lower they are in the stack order.
