# Develop Studio - Affinity Help Center

3.   Develop Studio

A Studio is a workspace that groups tools and panels specific to a design discipline or way of working. The Develop Studio allows full control over initial color, tonal and optical corrections of your RAW images. Additionally, switching between Pixel and Vector Studios allows you to re-develop images easily, which opens up additional editing and file-linking options.

![Image 1: Develop Studio in Affinity for desktop.](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/4MsDmOUeS7zbGGEupH7kMF/860d5a9b0b4c4ddb3ce499df248860e4/raw_develop_studio.jpg)

Develop Studio in Affinity for desktop.

Load a RAW image or **^(ctrl)**-click (Mac) / **right**-click (Windows) an image layer and select **Develop Studio**.

Here's an overview of the features of the Develop Studio.

*   Choice of Affinity or Apple Core Image RAW engines (Affinity for Windows only)
*   Remove automatic tone curve and apply your own
*   Automatic (and manual) lens correction and noise reduction
*   Focus Peaking feature that aids evaluation of focal points in an image prior to further editing
*   Develop Assistant settings for setting the default RAW engine, tonal treatment, lens correction, noise reduction, sharpening, exposure control behavior, and map region
*   Adobe Lens Correction Profile (LCP) support
*   ACM lens profile support
*   Dedicated Metadata, Lens, Details (noise) and Tones panels
*   Support for the latest cameras
*   Split view with syncing
*   Masks for restricting adjustments (object selection, brush, gradient, luminosity and hue range)
*   Show clipped highlights, shadows and tones
*   Hot pixel removal (Affinity RAW Engine only)

Adjustments can be applied globally (affecting the whole image) or locally (affecting only a selected area) with the use of dedicated masking tools. Here's a quick overview of some of the edits you may want to apply in the Develop Studio before taking your work further.

Color can be manipulated either with the dedicated tools (e.g., White Balance Tool

You can apply the initial **Tone Curve** onto your RAW images. The options available are a great starting point to further edits and allow you to open up the tones, or compress them, as required. Choose from the following:

*   **Standard**—offers a gentle boost in tonal contrast (default).
*   **Compressed**—offers less contrast with a subtle opening of the highlight tones by compressing the curve's end point.
*   **Natural**—offers gentle contrast with slightly more moderate opening of the shadows and highlights.
*   **High Contrast**—offers similar contrast treatment to the Standard option but with more highlight compression.
*   **Log**—compresses the tonal range so that highlights, midtones, and shadows are captured with more detail and less clipping. This creates a flat, low-contrast, low-saturation image that preserves maximum dynamic range, giving you far more flexibility to shape contrast, color, and brightness during editing.

Both split toning and black and white edits can be performed by manipulating a number of adjustments available on the **Tones** panel. Here, you can work on fine-tuning the contrast and tonality of your images.

You can also control the scene tones by selectively targeting only those areas that require attention. Find either the **Mask Gradient Tool** or **Mask Paint Tool**on the Masks panel, and use them to pre-select the areas to edit.

The Clarity and Texture controls in the Basic panel can be used to enhance perceived sharpness in RAW images before further editing. While both improve image detail, they operate at different scales:

*   **Clarity**increases midtone contrast across broader areas of the image. This enhances overall depth and structure, making edges and larger features appear more defined.
*   **Texture**enhances finer surface detail by increasing local (micro) contrast in midtones. It targets smaller features such as skin texture, fabric, and subtle patterns without significantly affecting overall contrast.

Because of their difference:

*   Use **Clarity**when you want to improve overall definition and add depth to the image.
*   Use **Texture**when you want to refine fine detail while maintaining a natural tonal appearance.

For best results, apply these adjustments subtly and in combination. Establish tonal balance first, then refine detail using Clarity or Texture as needed.

Images taken with various lenses may present a number of out-of-camera flaws not welcome in the final output. Using the Develop Studio's **Lens** panel, you can correct various aspects of optical imperfections.

Example: For lenses that offer wide aperture settings (_f_ 1.2, say) and creating a strong vignette as a result, you can correct the amount of under-exposure in the corners with **Remove Lens Vignette**'s dialog options. You can control the Intensity of this adjustment to set the correction to the level you are happy with.

Other panels available in the Develop Studio include 32-bit Preview for the extended dynamic range previews (desktop only), Metadata and History.
