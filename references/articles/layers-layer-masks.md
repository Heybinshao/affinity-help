3.   Layer masking

A layer mask is used to reveal or hide a portion of a layer. Masks are particularly useful for targeting specific areas of a composition and may be applied in a number of ways.

1.   On the **Layers** panel, select the layer you wish to mask.
2.   Click **Mask Layer**![Image 1](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/r2qPaYMZu1PUwNoo8Bluy/10da3cdaad29dff22d89069363829214/mask_layer_type.svg) .

The added mask will hide areas outside a selection (if a selection is in place) or display the entire layer (if no selection is in place).

*   On the **Layers** panel, select the layer you wish to mask. 
    *   For Mac: hold the **⌥** key and, at the bottom of the panel, click **Mask Layer**![Image 2](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/r2qPaYMZu1PUwNoo8Bluy/10da3cdaad29dff22d89069363829214/mask_layer_type.svg) , and then **Empty Mask** from the pop-up menu.
    *   For Windows: hold the **Alt** key and, at the bottom of the panel, click **Mask Layer**![Image 3](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/r2qPaYMZu1PUwNoo8Bluy/10da3cdaad29dff22d89069363829214/mask_layer_type.svg) .

1.   On the **Layers** panel, select the mask layer.
2.    Do one of the following: 
    *   To erase from the mask, paint on the page using the **Erase Brush Tool**![Image 4](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/1JePHOcZPgb6CwwmJi5c2b/14ccdc5a1fa7208f58fb9d35b264b302/eraser_tool.svg)  or the **Paint Brush Tool**![Image 5](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/6wRLtJhFhZTJ4aDIQnm8IX/9d71a0d96b84246e136cb94660a5ef88/paint_brush_tool.svg)  set to black foreground color.
    *   To restore the mask, paint on the page using the **Paint Brush Tool** set to white as its foreground color.
    *   Select the **Fill Tool**![Image 6](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/4AbVamuwkW3QLOJP3GQiV1/254daa0b4c76a58d6ef299e8068f0519/fill_tool.svg)  and drag across the layer. Adjust the gradient colors (context toolbar), as required.

As you erase on the pixel mask, adjust Size, Opacity, Flow and Hardness from the Erase Brush Tool's context toolbar.

Selecting a pixel mask's thumbnail automatically switches the **Color** panel to **Grayscale** mode.

The Erase Brush Tool always erases using 100% black (ignoring the current fill swatch).

Do one of the following:

*   With the layer and its mask(s) collapsed, **⇧**-click (Mac) / **Shift**-click (Windows) the mask. The hidden mask will show a diagonal red line across its thumbnail.
*   With the layer and its mask(s) expanded, toggle the mask layer's **Toggle Visibility**![Image 7](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/9rstenG65qWb7jzhC83X9/df6defa0ca6b00f2d4f82ec5823bffa6/visibility_on.svg) .

You can temporarily solo (isolate) a mask for editing by clicking its thumbnail while the **⌥** key (Mac) / **Alt** key (Windows) is pressed.

*   **^(ctrl)**-click (Mac) / **right**-click (Windows) the mask thumbnail and select an option from the pop-up menu.

Refining a mask may be particularly useful for targeted and subtle modifications to the pre-selected parts of your image.

In Affinity, two types of masking are possible:

*   **Pixel masking**: performs a similar task to the erase tools with one important difference: a pixel mask can be modified, or even discarded. Gradations of gray in a pixel mask can be used in various ways.
*   **Vector masking**: generated by a pen or shape tool. It involves using vector content as a mask over another layer that crops to the vector content's outline.

Masking can be applied at any level on the Layers panel—as an independent mask layer or applied to a specific layer (or child layer), to groups, or to individual objects. This is governed by the mask layer's positioning in the layer stack.

Masks are applied as separate layers, allowing you to freely edit and reposition them. They can be placed anywhere in the Layers panel—either as independent 'parent' mask layers or attached directly to specific layers or groups, known as [clipped](https://www.affinity.studio/help/layers-layer-clip/) child layers. A parent mask layer affects all layers below it in the stack.

By default, a new mask layer is clipped to the selected layer. If no layer is selected, the mask is added to the top of the Layers panel. You can freely reposition the mask within the Layers panel, giving you precise control over the areas it affects.

[Adjustment layers](https://www.affinity.studio/help/layers-adjustment-layers/) and [live filter layers](https://www.affinity.studio/help/layers-livefilters/) also include mask properties. You can reveal or hide areas affected by these layers in the same way as regular mask layers, allowing you to paint directly onto them without having to manually add separate masks.

Using masking is non-destructive, which means you can return to refine your masks at any given time, without any loss in image quality.

Mask layers can have a unique [blend mode](https://www.affinity.studio/help/layers-layer-blend-modes/) assigned, providing additional control over how target areas are affected.

Masking can be performed using a number of methods subject to how you wish to target your editing areas. Depending on the method, different tools are used to achieve masking in preparation for further work.

**Which mask type should I use?**

*   **Pixel selection mask**— use when you have an existing pixel selection to define the masked area.
*   **Grayscale painted mask**— use when you want to paint visibility on or off using black, white, or gray tonal values.
*   **Clipping mask**— use when you want to clip one layer's content to the outline of another.
*   **Luminosity mask (From Layer Intensity)**— use when you want to target tonal ranges based on the brightness values in your image.
*   **Channel mask**— use when you want to isolate and edit midtones, shadows, or highlights based on color channel data.
*   **Mask transparency**— use when you want to reduce the strength of a mask gradually rather than fully reveal or hide areas.

This technique, which is effective across various photographic genres, involves creating masks based on previously selected pixel areas.

1.   Make a selection using one of the [selection tools](https://www.affinity.studio/help/selections-selections-create/).
2.   Do one of the following: 
    *   On the **Layers** panel, click **Mask Layer**![Image 13](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/4q6OWVN4MyCGmeC0NuhiCI/696babeff770d207d6c4866c36cafb06/add_mask_layer.svg) .
    *   On the **Pixel** menu, select **New Mask Layer > Mask Layer**.

By default, a created mask layer is clipped to the selected layer. This behaviour can be changed in the app's **Settings > Assistant** section.

You can create a mask from a selection which has been 'painted' on your image using the [Quick Mask](https://www.affinity.studio/help/selections-edit-selection-as-layer/) mode.

You can use the **Pen Tool** to draw a closed shape that can be made into a mask. On the tool's context toolbar, tap **Mask**.

This type of masking, based on dark and light values, controls the visibility of the applied adjustment layer, filter layer, or similar elements. Grayscale masks work especially well with high-contrast images, giving you precise control over the visibility level of adjustments or filters.

1.   Select an already applied adjustment layer or filter layer.
2.   Do one of the following: 
    *   Use the **Paint Brush Tool**![Image 14](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/6wRLtJhFhZTJ4aDIQnm8IX/9d71a0d96b84246e136cb94660a5ef88/paint_brush_tool.svg)  set to varied levels of gray, 50%, say.
    *   Use the **Fill Tool**![Image 15](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/4AbVamuwkW3QLOJP3GQiV1/254daa0b4c76a58d6ef299e8068f0519/fill_tool.svg)  and set the start and end points' tonal values to varied levels of gray e.g., white and black on the opposite ends.

A clipping (or revealing) mask allows parts of the topmost layer to become visible while hiding areas of the bottom (base) layer. Common examples include images clipped inside text or within drawn shapes.

1.   Open an image.
2.   Create an object, e.g., text or a shape, and ensure it is selected.
3.    Do one of the following: 
    *   Drag the shape layer onto the thumbnail of the image layer you want to clip to.
    *   **⌃(ctrl)**-click (Mac) / **right**-click (Windows) the layer to clip (shape, text, etc.) and choose **Create Clipping Mask**. 
    *   On the **Layer** menu, select **Create Clipping Mask**.

The thumbnail of the target object changes to indicate that a clipping mask and crop have been applied using a "crop to top object" operation.

You can target specific tonal areas of an image based on l _uminosity_ (relative brightness). The selected regions are based on the gray tonal values of an image, not color.

1.   On the **Pixel** menu, choose **Pixel Selection > From Layer Intensity**.
2.   On the **Layers** panel, click **Mask Layer**![Image 16](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/r2qPaYMZu1PUwNoo8Bluy/10da3cdaad29dff22d89069363829214/mask_layer_type.svg) .

This masking method is particularly useful for targeting midtones, shadows and highlights of an image based on its color channels information, where you can separate and edit these areas non-destructively.

1.   Select the image's layer.
2.   Choose **Pixel > Pixel Selection > Tonal Range** and decide on either: **Midtones**, **Shadows** or **Highlights**.
3.   Choose **Pixel > Pixel Selection > Save > As Spare Channel**.
4.   On the **Channels** panel, **^(ctrl)**-click (Mac) / **right**-click (Windows) **Pixel Selection** and select **Create Spare Channel**. You can rename this depending on the selected tonal range. 
5.   Repeat the above for other tonal values, as required.

Try adding live filters to the newly-created channels and observe how they target only the relevant areas of your image.

Setting different levels of transparency to a layer mask controls how much of the effect, adjustment or other editing is visible (see-through).

1.   Select a mask layer.
2.   On top of the **Layers** panel, change the selected layer mask's **Opacity** value.

In addition to using brushes to reveal or conceal the effects of adjustments, filters, or color effects on a mask, the **Clear Mask** and **Fill Mask** options provide a quick and easy way to modify your edits.

1.   Select a mask layer.
2.   On the **Layers** panel, **⌃(ctrl)**-click (Mac) / **right**-click (Windows) the mask icon to access the option.
