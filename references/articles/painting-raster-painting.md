Affinity comes packed with brush choices for numerous creative applications—all available in the Brushes panel.

Each category in the **Brushes** panel contains brushes of varying properties and characteristics and they can be edited to suit individual creative needs. Additionally, new brushes can be created from scratch or imported (.abr and .afbrushes formats) from a number of sources.

The appearance of brush strokes are determined by a combination of the chosen brush tool, the Brushes panel and the tool's context toolbar.

Using the **Symmetry** and **Mirror** options found on the context toolbar, you can create variable repeating patterns with radial or Mandala-style symmetry. See [Symmetry and Mirror](https://www.affinity.studio/help/painting-symmetry-brushes/) for more information.

If you apply a raster brush stroke to a selected vector object, a new pixel layer will be created that is automatically [clipped](https://www.affinity.studio/help/layers-layer-clip/) to the vector object. Clipping helps to contain textures within object boundaries.

Use your keyboard's left or right arrow keys to manually rotate your brush nozzle(s) before and during stroke application.

1.   Use the **Layers** panel to select the layer that you want to work on, or create a new layer.
2.   In the Pixel Studio ![Image 8](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/x0qUKV0QmljBpXjlKaAyD/48b1503c7d6cc8f05d91c6af70f31b85/persona_photo.svg) , select the **Paint Brush Tool**![Image 9](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/6wRLtJhFhZTJ4aDIQnm8IX/9d71a0d96b84246e136cb94660a5ef88/paint_brush_tool.svg)  or other brush-based tool.
3.   Do one of the following: 
    *   On the **Brushes** panel, select a brush of your choice.
    *   To continue with a previously used brush (current session only): On the **Layers** panel, select **Recent brushes**![Image 10](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/7HxpRtMyF1dKpVk78Z0ZZE/6c4c14b63bb1f9686949de09071ed820/brush_on_layer.svg)  on the layer entry and select a brush thumbnail from the pop-up menu.

4.   Adjust the context toolbar settings on the context toolbar or right-click on the page to access additional options. 
5.   Select a stroke color from the **Color** panel.
6.   Drag on the page in the direction that you want the brush stroke to follow.

1.   Select a brush tool.
2.   Press a numerical key, or two numerical keys in quick succession, to set the opacity. For example: 
    *   Press **4** for 40% opacity.
    *   Press **0** for 100% opacity.
    *   Press **4** and **5** for 45% opacity.
    *   Press **0** and **7** for 7% opacity.

To change flow instead, hold the **⌃(ctrl)** key (Mac) or hold the right mouse button (Windows) and press a number key.

*   On the context toolbar, enable the **Stabilizer** option and choose one of the following: 
    *   **Rope mode**—drag the stroke end by a 'rope' that smooths the stroke but lets you introduce sharp corners at increasing **Length** (radius) values by redirecting the slackened rope.
    *   **Window mode**—smooths the stroke by averaging the stroke's position over a **Window** whose size is configurable.

*   In the Pixel Studio ![Image 11](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/x0qUKV0QmljBpXjlKaAyD/48b1503c7d6cc8f05d91c6af70f31b85/persona_photo.svg) , select the **Undo Brush Tool**![Image 12](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/4YfcEAr6dIBz22Hh8y1MFx/0c24b41846c2e73422707e13b8ceffd2/undo_brush_tool.svg)  and paint over the areas previously painted to remove strokes.
*   On the **History** panel, scroll the list and select the camera icon that corresponds with the history entry that you want to paint back to. Alternatively, you can use the slider located under the history list and drag it left to undo your strokes.
*   Paint over pixels which you want to return to their original value.

2.   Adjust the context toolbar settings, as required.
3.   Paint on the page in the direction that you want the erase brush stroke to follow.

This tool works best if you've added brush strokes non-destructively to a separate layer instead of your main image layer.

*   Tap the left or right arrow keys to adjust the rotation of the nozzle.

The following can be used:

*   To draw a straight brush stroke, lay down your initial stroke, then **⇧**-click (Mac) / **Shift**-click (Windows) at the position where your stroke is to end; keep the key pressed to continue the straight line stroke.
*   To constrain a stroke to the X-axis or Y-axis, press the **⇧** key (Mac) / **Shift** key (Windows) immediately after your initial stroke is laid down, then drag along the X or Y axis to the position where your stroke is to end; keep the key pressed to continue the stroke.
*   Press the **⌥** key (Mac) / **Alt** key (Windows) and hold down the left mouse button, then drag to pick up a new color under the brush to paint with.
*   Pressing **⌥** key (Mac) / **Alt** key (Windows) while selecting a new raster brush from the Brushes panel ignores size and the associated tool (if set).
*   To ignore only the associated tool while selecting a brush from the panel, press **⌥⇧** keys (Mac) / **Alt**+**Shift** keys (Windows).
*   Press the **⌃(ctrl)⌥** keys (Mac) / **Ctrl**+**Alt** keys (Windows) and: 
    *   Click to cycle between size and hardness, shape and spacing, and rotation attributes.
    *   Drag left/right or up/down to adjust the corresponding attribute. For example, with size and hardness attributes selected, drag left/right to adjust the first attribute and up/down to adjust the second attribute.

Related behaviors can be adjusted from the app's settings:

*   **Assistant > Brush tool sharing**. Use to switch brush tools, while maintaining the selected brush type.
*   **User Interface > Show brush previews**
*   **User Interface > Always show brush crosshair**
