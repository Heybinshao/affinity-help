The White Balance adjustment

 allows you to remove unwanted color casts or give your composition a warmer or cooler feel.

![Image 3: Black & White before](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/5NXgmzFzXKve50c596UktV/3587c26a6e00f462f4d50c450a098ea1/adjustment_base.jpg)

Typically, **White Balance** is available as an adjustment layer. Additionally, in the Develop Studio however, it appears on the **Basic** panel and also as a dedicated **White Balance Tool** on the toolbar.

*   **White Balance**—controls the 'temperature'. Drag the slider to the left to reduce warm tones (by adding a blue tint), drag the slider to the right to increase them (by adding an amber tint).
*   **Tint**—tints either towards magenta or green.
*   **Picker**—allows you to sample to set the white point on which the white balance will be calculated. You can sample using one of the following methods: 
    *   Click to sample a pixel under the cursor.
    *   Drag the cursor across the image to sample all pixels under the cursor. The averaged color from those pixels is used.
    *    Press the **⌥** key (Mac) / **Alt** key (Windows) and drag to sample under a rectangular marquee, again using the averaged color of those pixels. 

In the Develop Studio, a dedicated **White Point Tool** is used instead of the **Picker**. It offers the same sampling functionality as the Picker.

In the Develop Studio, the image uses the camera's "As Shot" White Balance values by default. Checking the adjustment's checkbox displays those values, which can be reverted to at any time by unchecking the adjustment's checkbox.

In the Pixel Studio:

*   On the **Layers** panel, click **Adjustments**![Image 4](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/4ZFx9I9M7MiUBPmytBqZrt/cad5a29a5e39b1b3faa42c5fa29402f3/add_adjustment_layer.svg)  and select **White Balance**.
*   On the **Adjustment** panel, select **White Balance**![Image 5](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/7vRaaVeQyacUhpWGE2lyrj/d861bb177f602639bb9f9a1675ca2bef/white_balance_adjustment_type.svg)  and then click on a preset to modify, as required.
*   On the **Pixel** menu, select **New Adjustment Layer > White Balance**.

The **Adjustment** panel is not visible by default. To enable it, navigate to the top menu and select **Window > Pixel > Adjustment**.

**Auto White Balance** is also available from the top toolbar.

In the Develop Studio:

*   On the **Basic** panel, use the dedicated controls.
*   From the toolbar, select the **White Balance Tool**![Image 6](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/3eLlasmxSbwxcGXOjVEP30/e5e435e9799956b354718603b31e23f1/white_balance_tool.svg)  and then click on a neutral area (closest to gray) in the image to establish correct white balance.
