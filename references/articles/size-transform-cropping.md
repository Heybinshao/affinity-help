3.   Cropping and straightening images

Cropping removes unwanted areas of your image for either practical reasons or better composition.

Cropping is used for practical or aesthetic reasons: for example, an unwanted object or person can be excluded from the frame to balance image composition and make it more appealing to the eye.

Affinity lets you crop in Unconstrained, Original or Custom aspect ratios. For print or web delivery, you can specify common print sizes (e.g., 6" x 4") or create pixel-accurate custom crop regions.

Snapping and Guides can facilitate cropping operations. See the dedicated topics to find out more.

Cropping affects the entire canvas and when applied, the regions outside the drawn crop area are hidden. However, cropping is non-destructive, which means you can always uncrop your image at a later stage.

The context toolbar's **Mode** options for crop operations are:

*   **Unconstrained**—the crop area can be controlled freely.
*   **Original Ratio**—retains your image's original aspect ratio.
*   **Custom Ratio**—uses the adjacent input boxes to set the ratio—width in the left box, height in the right box. A modified ratio can be saved as a preset.
*   **Resample**—use the adjacent **Width** and **Height** settings to set dimensions for the crop area at your chosen measurement **Units** and **DPI**. This is a great way to crop to a chosen print size (e.g. 10in x 8in) with print-ready resolution, e.g. a DPI of 300.

You can crop to absolute pixel dimensions by entering pixel width and height values adjacent to the **Mode** setting.

*   **Thirds Grid**—shown by default, the Rule of Thirds' intersection lines can be positioned over an object of interest in your image.
*   **Golden Spiral**—also knows as the Fibonacci Spiral or divine proportions, the mode aids in balancing compositions naturally.
*   **Diagonals**—aids positioning two or more objects of interest under the diagonal line intersections to balance them.
*   **Phi Grid**—unlike the Rule of Thirds, this grid doesn't divide the frame into nine equal frames; instead, only the top and bottom segments are the same.

The following can be used:

*    Holding down **⇧** (Mac) / **Shift** (Windows) while cropping in the **Unconstrained** mode constrains the current aspect ratio. 
*    The left and right arrow keys can be used to nudge the area in single-pixel increments. Holding down **⇧** (Mac) / **Shift** (Windows) while pressing the left and right arrow keys nudges the crop area in 10-pixel increments. 
*    For Mac: Holding down the **⌘** key and dragging on the page (not using handles) toggles the crop area overlay and enters the **Straighten** mode. When pressed while cropping using one of the handles, the tool's bounding box allows to recompose the image around its center. 
*    For Windows: Holding down the **Ctrl** key and dragging on the page (not one of the handles) toggles the crop area overlay and enters the **Straighten** mode. When pressed while cropping using one of the handles, the tool's bounding box allows to recompose the image around its center. 
*   You can quickly toggle between overlay modes by pressing **O**.
