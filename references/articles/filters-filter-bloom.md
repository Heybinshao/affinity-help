A bloom or glow effect can be a valuable addition to any image editing workflow, especially in photography—including infrared—and 3D rendering. Beyond enhancing visual impact, the Bloom filter can replicate the distinctive glow produced by vintage lenses, where highlights appear softened and diffused. This rendering style evokes a nostalgic, character-rich aesthetic often associated with film photography, giving images a timeless, expressive quality.

The bloom effect, alongside other image manipulation methods, can also help achieve better dynamic range in your photos.

By adjusting the filter settings, you can replicate the visual aesthetics of cinematic filters. These effects soften tones and subtly reduce color contrast, creating a dreamy look. The result is imagery rich in character, atmosphere, and expressive mood often reminiscent of movie scenes.

The **Bloom** filter can also be loaded onto the **Filter Brush Tool**, allowing you to selectively apply the effect to specific areas of your image.

Use varied levels of layer **Opacity** to fine tune the filter effect or, when applying strokes with the **Filter Brush Tool**, low Flow to gradually build the desired effect.

To use the destructive version of the filter, select it from the **Pixel > Filters > Blur** menu. The live version can be accessed from:

*   the **Layers** panel by clicking **Live Filters**![Image 4](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/2ALwfgZXKqtubPaMdMHvlC/d4901c280214d88174d57188a9c56e21/filters_studio.svg) .
*   the **Pixel > New Live Filter Layer > Blur** menu.

*   **Method**—choose between Contrast and Bright methods. Modifications with the former method selected produce punchier and more colorful effects, while the later result in brighter, less saturated output.
*   **Shadow / Midtone / Highlight Blend**—control how much of the bloom effect is added to shadows, midtones and highlights, respectively.
*   **Color**—controls how much of the bloom effect is added to the overal saturation.
*   **Strong**—when checked, increases the overall effect.
*   **Opacity**—controls how transparent the effect is.
*   **Blend Mode**—controls how the effect blends with the layers beneath it.
