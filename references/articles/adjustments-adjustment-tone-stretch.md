The Tone Stretch adjustment

![Image 1](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/5GpmnCdRXWfdZnVck2YPaX/392a557bfdd0c5a2954beebd811e8547/tone_stretch.svg)

 enables non-destructive editing of your astrophotography by stretching the tonal range of stacked images.

![Image 2: After](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/24V9ewOjYkgX8cyVAHzC73/092b906eb5f16c09a4ac1ffd784aa23d/adjustment_toneStretch_after.jpg)

![Image 3: Before](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/36NpjDfA5Ag3nqbAvvBjex/a8798a8867aa1a23ae01bc05d668e166/adjustment_toneStretch_before.jpg)

When photographing stars, constellations, and celestial bodies, exposure stacking is commonly employed to enhance the signal-to-noise ratio and achieve accurate scene reproduction. However, this technique often leads to images that appear quite dark, necessitating adjustments to bring out meaningful details. By stretching these images, you can create a more balanced visual representation of the scene, allowing for further modifications to meet your desired output.

One of the major benefits of this adjustment is its integrated tone compression feature. In astrophotography, managing stretches is crucial to avoid clipping or overexposure, especially in bright regions such as stars or nebula cores. Affinity simplifies this process by automatically applying tone compression after each stretch adjustment, resulting in a well-balanced outcome without necessitating additional effort from the user.

Appropriate astrophotography source imagery is required for the adjustment to work. This could be obtained from:

*   Traditional camera able to capture images in RAW file format (DNG, CR2, ARW, etc.).
*   Dedicated astrophotography camera that captures files in the FIT file format (Flexible Image Transport System)

*   **Method**—sets the tonal stretch type. Choose from: 
    *   **Basic**—a standard screen transfer stretch, brightening the image.
    *   **Arcsinh**—producing a gentle logarithmic stretch that preserves relative color intensity.
    *   **Logarithmic**—producing a gentle stretch with slightly more highlight compression compared to Arcsinh.
    *   **Structural**—producing a more aggressive stretch with brighter highlights.
    *   **Color Preserving**—producing an aggressive stretch that additionally retains more color intensity.

*   **Gamma**—controls the intensity of color compression.
*   **Stretch Factor**—controls the intensity of the overall stretch.
*   **Compression**—controls the level of tonal compression applied to the stretched tones. Compression at 0% will present a punchier result with more contrast whereas at 100% it will compress harder, dampening bright highlights and bringing the black level up slightly.

Do one of the following:

*   On the Layers panel, click **Adjustments**![Image 4](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/4ZFx9I9M7MiUBPmytBqZrt/cad5a29a5e39b1b3faa42c5fa29402f3/add_adjustment_layer.svg)  and select **Tone Stretch**.
*   On the **Adjustment** panel, select **Tone Stretch**![Image 5](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/5GpmnCdRXWfdZnVck2YPaX/392a557bfdd0c5a2954beebd811e8547/tone_stretch.svg)  and then click on a preset to modify, as required.
*   On the **Pixel** menu, select **New Adjustment Layer > Tone Stretch**.

The **Adjustment** panel is not visible by default. To enable it, navigate to the top menu and select **Window > Pixel > Adjustment**.
