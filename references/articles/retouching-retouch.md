3.   Retouching

Retouching techniques utilize a selection of brush-based tools for focused color and tonal adjustment.

![Image 1: Retouching techniques after](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/3WYptNuOYMUkjHB908SVJn/e4f3893755cb28e28e5c566ea70723ca/retouching_after.png)

After: Dodging (A), Burning (B), Sponging (C), Blurring (D), Sharpening (E), Denoising - Median (F) and Smudging (G).

![Image 2: Retouching techniques before](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/1lvEx4qSyHr9P00Xw0VOzh/86ce45fed46dce48ea8edf3d2ef44137/retouching_before.png)

Before: Dodging (A), Burning (B), Sponging (C), Blurring (D), Sharpening (E), Denoising - Median (F) and Smudging (G).

In contrast to the adjustment layers, the retouch brushes apply modifications to a selected pixel layer, thereby affecting individual pixels on the layer.

The retouch brushes provide the opportunity for precision work in editing images and pixel layers. For example, the **Dodge Brush Tool** is ideal for lightening areas of shadow within a photo while leaving other areas unaffected.

#### The retouch brushes available in the Pixel Studio 

![Image 3](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/x0qUKV0QmljBpXjlKaAyD/48b1503c7d6cc8f05d91c6af70f31b85/persona_photo.svg)

 include

*   ![Image 4](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/24qfalDe2RscIbJxr7VAhZ/a3dfadf0d5673b60dd0f0373d0e51fc7/adjustment_brush_tool.svg) **Adjustment Brush Tool**—allows to paint over image areas with a pre-loaded adjustment of choice.
*   ![Image 5](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/4qDqeDO6CwV5ff8OcWn5Na/0568c064a1468d7010fcc540d302289f/filter_brush.svg) **Filter Brush Tool**—allows to paint over image areas with a pre-loaded filter of choice.
*   ![Image 6](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/5QPGu2iGfXQE4VUebZa70M/a46fe1835355d4da1b2ea6239caff901/dodge_tool.svg) **Dodge Brush Tool**—dodging adjusts the exposure under the brush stroke to lighten the painted area. This has the opposite effect to Burning.
*   ![Image 7](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/2SEF1Vp1gpr8Gtt7tb9MSy/4c01a454f9118170d31fb2ceb4bd07c0/burn_brush_tool.svg) **Burn Brush Tool**—burning adjusts the exposure under the brush stroke to darken the painted area. This has the opposite effect to Dodging.
*   ![Image 8](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/2qPnMRlMX17p2PqCNazjlj/2ac51756cf74aaed4d65a011dba19a06/sponge_brush_tool.svg) **Sponge Brush Tool**—sponging lets you increase or decrease the color saturation under the brush.
*   ![Image 9](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/4qOy7CwJvKYkjsVxK6rqXH/c9819c392a301f040f80fa5f5392615d/blur_brush_tool.svg) **Blur Brush Tool**—blurring reduces the contrast between pixels under the brush stroke, thereby softening the edges of the painted area. This has the opposite effect to Sharpening. Blurring does not smear color within the painted area like Smudging.
*   ![Image 10](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/1hwKoRHleQOBisFsesdoro/807a2a5358e2617c9e7355466feb51c3/sharpen_brush_tool.svg) **Sharpen Brush Tool**—sharpening increases the contrast between pixels under the brush stroke, thereby enhancing the edges of the painted area. This has the opposite effect to Blurring.
*   ![Image 11](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/33aS0M6Dn3aqLVgRSM8DLn/58a2de043d5c29ae796ed98c3792dabe/median_brush_tool.svg) **Median Brush Tool**—painted pixels are blended together to reduce noise under the stroke.
*   ![Image 12](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/7CboG2Abo847lPpt0DVWqm/73db7999cd00407038bf82da6cb85685/smudge_brush_tool.svg) **Smudge Brush Tool**—smudging is a technique which allows you to blend pixels within an image. The brush 'picks up' color from the click point and 'drags' it in the brush stroke direction.

Don't forget other retouching tools such as Cloning and Healing Brush tools, Blemish Removal Tool, and Inpainting Brush Tool. You'll also be able to use the Patch Tool and Red Eye Removal Tool.

Affinity conveniently organises all the tools and panels you need for compositing workflows in its own **Retouching Studio**

![Image 13: Retouching Studio](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/55Tejv2qP2VzWf3iD9YFZT/7c2a5b825dd1a0d393aa1c9073992306/retouching_studio.svg)

. To learn more about it, visit the dedicated Help topic.

1.   Use the **Layers** panel to select the pixel layer that you want to work on.
2.   Select a retouch brush tool.
3.   The tool uses a soft-round brush by default. To use a different brush style, choose one from the **Brushes** panel.
4.   On the context toolbar, change the brush values as desired.
5.   Drag on the image to apply the effect under the brush stroke.
