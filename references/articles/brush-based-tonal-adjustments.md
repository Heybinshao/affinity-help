3.   Brush-based tonal adjustments

Brush-based tonal editing allows you to refine specific areas of an image with precision. Unlike global adjustments, which affect the entire layer, brush tools let you target shadows, midtones, highlights, or color intensity exactly where needed.

Brush-based workflows are particularly effective in portrait retouching and compositing workflows, where subtle, controlled adjustments help improve depth and tonal consistency.

Use brush-based methods when you need to:

*   Brighten or darken specific areas
*   Enhance local contrast
*   Subtly increase or reduce color intensity
*   Smooth tonal transitions
*   Blend composited elements naturally
*   Refine edges or soften distractions

These brush tools modify tonal or color values directly in the areas you paint:

*   **Tone Brush Tool**—lets you selectively adjust brightness, contrast, and color in specific areas of your image.
*   **Dodge Brush Tool**—lightens painted areas to enhance highlights, increase perceived depth, or draw attention to key details.
*   **Burn Brush Tool**—darkens painted areas to strengthen shadows, improve contrast, or subtly sculpt form.
*   **Sponge Brush Tool**—increases or decreases color saturation locally, allowing you to enhance vibrancy or reduce oversaturation without affecting the entire image.
*   **Paint Mixer Brush Tool**—blends existing tones and colors together to soften transitions and smooth gradients. It is particularly useful for skin retouching or integrating composited edges.
*   **Color Replacement Tool**—replaces color in painted areas while preserving underlying luminance, making it suitable for correcting color shifts without significantly altering tonal structure.
*   **Blur Brush Tool**—softens tonal transitions and reduces local contrast. It is useful for smoothing harsh edges or reducing distracting detail.
*   **Sharpen Brush Tool**—increases local contrast to enhance detail and clarity. It is best applied selectively to avoid over-sharpening.
*   **Median Brush Tool**—reduces small tonal inconsistencies and noise while preserving edge definition. It is helpful in retouching and texture cleanup workflows.
*   **Smudge Brush Tool**—pushes and blends pixel values to redistribute tones manually. It can be used for subtle corrections or creative tonal shaping.

Brush-based adjustments are most effective when applied gradually using low flow settings and evaluated alongside global tonal balance. They are best suited to deal with the following:

*   Brighten or darken specific image areas.
*   Enhance local contrast.
*   Smooth tonal transitions.
*   Refine edges.
*   Reduce noise in selected areas.
*   Harmonize tones in composited images.

1.   On the **Layers** panel, select the target layer.
2.   Select one of the tools listed above for tonal editing, as required.
3.   On the context toolbar, adjust tool options.
4.   Start painting to correct tonal inconsistencies.
5.   (Optional) Use layer **Blend Modes** and **Blend Options** for fine control over the affected areas.
6.   (Optional) Use layer **Opacity**, if required, to reduce your edits impact.

*   Use low flow and build adjustments gradually.
*   Work non-destructively on duplicate layers.
*   Compare before and after results by toggling layer visibility.
*   Avoid excessive brightening or darkening that may clip detail.
*   Combine local edits with global tonal adjustments for balanced results.
