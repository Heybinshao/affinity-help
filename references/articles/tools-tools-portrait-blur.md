1.   In the **Canva AI Studio**, on the **Layers** panel, select the image layer you want to edit.
2.   Select the **Portrait Blur Tool**![Image 4](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/5UgE2oVQrEKG1NIA0sRL5q/dec2aab8057dd14fab97e2f65af919c5/portrait_blur.svg) .
3.   On the window that appears:

*   Adjust **Radius**to control the strength of the blur.
*   Adjust **Focal Distance**to set the area of the image that should remain in focus.
*   Adjust **Blur Smoothness**to control how gradually the blur transitions across the image.

4. (Optional) Use the preview controls to compare the result against the original image.

5. Click **Apply**.

The Portrait Blur Tool does not have a default keyboard shortcut, but you can assign one manually if desired. See '[Customizing keyboard shortcuts](https://www.affinity.studio/help/workspace-customizing-shortcuts/)' for details.

The effect this Tool offers is one akin to that of shallow depth of field often opted for in fine art or studio portraiture. It is used to separate the subject from the background to give it more focus and separation.

While many portrait sessions are often conducted in highly controlled conditions, there are situations where it's not possible to photograph a subject with lens set to low aperture settings (_f_ 1.8 or lower, for example) to separate it from the background. This might be due to an abundance of available light and/or the absence of shaded areas, such as in the image above. For situations like that, the Portrait Blur Tool allows for that same effect to be simulated.

Other examples where the tool may be useful are product or food photography, where focus and attention is brought to a small part of the subject.

The Portrait Blur Tool is also a great alternative to a lengthy, manual workflow where careful subject selections and refining is required to create close to optically convincing results. Instead, the dialog options for the Portrait Blur Tool in Affinity ensure precise transition control over the blurred areas thus simulating natural looking outputs.

The following settings can be adjusted:

*   **Radius**—determines the radius of the blur. While the slider ranges from 0 to 32px, it is possible to enter the value manually by clicking inside the field.
*   **Focal Distance**—simulates the distance between the lens and the camera sensor. Lower values correspond to a closer distance (resulting in shallower depth of field) simulation while higher ones do the opposite (resulting in wider depth of field).
*   **Blur Smoothness**—controls how smooth the transition between the out-of-focus and in-focus area is.
