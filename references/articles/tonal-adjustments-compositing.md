# Tonal adjustments in compositing

When combining elements from multiple sources, tonal consistency is essential for a natural result. Differences in lighting direction, contrast, and dynamic range can make composited elements appear disconnected.

![Image 4: Example of a compositing project in Affinity with the Layers panel exposed.
](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/4r8uJmkvdxpZpKXGjj0Ep/47f7a471687ff3b20d17cf69dc6e688a/tonality_compositing.jpg)

Example of a compositing project in Affinity with the Layers panel exposed.

[](https://www.affinity.studio/help/tonal-adjustments-compositing/#establish-global-tonal-balance)

## Establish global tonal balance

Tonal adjustments in compositing workflows are used to harmonize shadows, midtones, and highlights so that all layers share a consistent tonal structure across the whole image. Establishing a balanced tonal foundation helps prevent inconsistencies during later refinements. Consider the following global adjustments:

*   Curves to refine overall contrast and tonal separation.
*   Levels to set consistent black and white points across layers.
*   Shadows/Highlights to recover lost detail in extreme tonal areas.

[](https://www.affinity.studio/help/tonal-adjustments-compositing/#match-tonal-range-between-layers)

#### Match tonal range between layers

Matching tonal range is often more important than matching color at this stage. Imported elements may have different exposure or contrast characteristics. To align them consider the following:

*   Adjust individual layers using **Curves** or **Levels** .
*   Use **Blend Options** to limit adjustments to specific tonal ranges.
*   Compare shadow depth and highlight intensity between layers.

[](https://www.affinity.studio/help/tonal-adjustments-compositing/#refine-locally)

#### Refine locally

Local refinement improves integration and reduces visible seams. After global alignment, refine transitions between elements.

*   Use **Dodge/Burn Brush Tool** to shape light direction and depth.
*   Use the **Tone Brush Tool** to blend tones by sampling underlying content.
*   Use the **Blur Brush Tool** or the **Paint Mixer Brush Tool** to soften tonal edges.

[](https://www.affinity.studio/help/tonal-adjustments-compositing/#group-tonal-control)

#### Group tonal control

Grouped tonal adjustments simplify complex composites. Because compositing involves bringing in and combining a number of assets together, and because they differ in their tonality, it is challenging to blend them all in together in a realistic way. Affinity's **Live Tone Blend Group** feature ensures light blending of the composite elements is harmonized with the rest of the scene thus creating a more believable scene.

[](https://www.affinity.studio/help/tonal-adjustments-compositing/#how-to-edit-tones-using-grouped-toning)

### How to edit tones using grouped toning

Desktop app

1.   On the **Layers**panel, select the layers corresponding to the composite elements.
2.   Access the **Live Tone Blend Group** using one of the following methods:
    1.   on the **Pixel**menu, select **Live Tone Blend Group**.
    2.   right-click on the page and select **Live Tone Blend Group**.
    3.   in the Compositing Studio, on the top bar, select **Live Tone Blend Group**  ![Image 5: Live Tone Blend Group](https://images.ctfassets.net/3p2fxa94bzao/4QwnwbtLzNdMFmosyTP9zQ/eba11ef9ff6694eff2139b714bc5cdd7/live_tone_blend_group.svg)       .

3.   On the dialog, adjust the options.
4.   Close the dialog.

[](https://www.affinity.studio/help/tonal-adjustments-compositing/#best-practices)

#### Best practices

*   Match contrast before adjusting color.
*   Maintain consistent shadow density across the composite.
*   Avoid excessive global contrast that reduces layer visibility.
*   Toggle layer visibility to compare before and after adjustments.
