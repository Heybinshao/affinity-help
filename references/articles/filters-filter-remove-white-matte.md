# Remove White Matte filter

The Remove White Matte filter removes white fringing from cutout subjects placed on a new background.

## About the Remove White Matte filter

White fringing typically occurs when background removal tools leave semi-transparent edge pixels that retain the original white background color, or when imported assets use premultiplied alpha — where transparency is blended against white by default. It can also appear in JPEG-format cutouts, where compression blends edge pixels with the original background color.

Desktop app

This filter can be found on the **Pixel > Filters > Colors** menu.

This filter has no customizable settings.

**Which matte filter should I use?**

*   **White fringing**— use the Remove White Matte filter when edge pixels retain a white or light color from the original background.
*   **Black or dark fringing**— use the [Remove Black Matte filter](https://www.affinity.studio/help/filters-filter-remove-black-matte/) when edge pixels retain a dark color from the original background.
*   **Colored or complex fringing**— use [Refine pixel selection edges](https://www.affinity.studio/help/selections-selections-refine/) for greater control over edge quality, especially for subjects with fine detail such as hair or fur.

**If fringing persists**

The Remove White Matte filter works best on thin, uniform fringing. If the white fringe is wide, the original cutout quality is poor, or your subject has fine edge detail, the filter may not fully clean the edge. In these cases, try [selection refinement](https://www.affinity.studio/help/selections-selections-refine/) to rework the mask edge, or manually paint the mask to refine affected areas.
